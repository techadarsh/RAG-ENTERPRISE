1. POLICY STATEMENT

Our organization is committed to providing a workplace free from sexual harassment and ensuring the safety, dignity, and respect of all employees regardless of gender, sexual orientation, or gender identity. This policy is established in compliance with the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013.

Zero Tolerance Approach:
- We maintain a zero-tolerance policy toward sexual harassment
- All complaints will be treated seriously and investigated promptly
- Confidentiality will be maintained throughout the process
- No retaliation against complainants or witnesses
- Swift and appropriate action against offenders

3. DEFINITION OF SEXUAL HARASSMENT

Sexual harassment includes any unwelcome sexually determined behavior, whether directly or by implication:

A. Physical Contact and Advances:
   - Unwelcome physical touching, patting, or inappropriate contact
   - Blocking someone's path or cornering
   - Deliberate invasion of personal space
   - Physical assault or attempts thereof

B. Verbal Harassment:
   - Sexual comments, jokes, or innuendos
   - Requests or demands for sexual favors
   - Sexually colored remarks about appearance, clothing, or body
   - Derogatory comments about gender or sexual orientation
   - Persistent unwanted romantic or sexual attention

C. Non-Verbal Harassment:
   - Display of sexually explicit materials (posters, screensavers, emails)
   - Leering, staring, or making obscene gestures
   - Sending sexually explicit messages, photos, or videos
   - Stalking or following someone persistently

D. Quid Pro Quo Harassment:
   - Implied or explicit promise of preferential treatment in return for sexual favors
   - Threats of adverse employment action for refusing sexual advances
   - Making employment decisions based on submission to or rejection of unwelcome conduct

E. Hostile Work Environment:
   - Creating an intimidating, hostile, or offensive work environment
   - Repeated unwelcome advances despite clear rejection
   - Gender-based discrimination or exclusion
   - Retaliation against someone who rejected advances

5. COMPLAINT FILING PROCESS

Step 1: Filing a Complaint
Complaints can be filed through multiple channels:
- Written complaint to ICC (email/physical letter)
- Anonymous reporting through ICC portal
- Verbal complaint to any ICC member (will be documented)
- Complaint box located in HR office

Complaint Requirements:
 Name and contact details of complainant (unless anonymous)
 Name and designation of respondent
 Description of incident(s) with dates, times, and locations
 Names of witnesses (if any)
 Any supporting evidence (emails, messages, documents)

Timeline: Complaints should be filed within 3 months of the incident. Extension may be granted for valid reasons.

Step 2: Preliminary Inquiry
- ICC acknowledges complaint within 2 working days
- Preliminary inquiry conducted within 7 days
- Decision on whether to proceed with formal inquiry

Step 3: Formal Investigation
- Respondent provided with copy of complaint
- Both parties allowed to present their case with evidence
- Witnesses examined
- Investigation completed within 90 days
- Extension of 90 days may be granted for complex cases

Step 4: ICC Report and Action
- ICC submits detailed report with recommendations
- Management implements recommendations within 60 days
- Disciplinary action may include:
  * Written warning
  * Suspension without pay
  * Transfer or relocation
  * Demotion or salary reduction
  * Termination of employment
  * Legal action in severe cases

6. CONFIDENTIALITY AND PRIVACY

Protection Measures:
 All proceedings conducted in strict confidentiality
 Information shared only on need-to-know basis
 No disclosure of complainant or witness identity without consent
 Separate communication channels for sensitive matters
 Secure storage of complaint records

Breach Consequences:
Any breach of confidentiality by ICC members or others will result in disciplinary action, which may include termination.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

7. PROTECTION AGAINST RETALIATION

Anti-Retaliation Safeguards:
- No adverse employment action against complainants or witnesses
- Complainant cannot be transferred without written consent
- Performance evaluations must be fair and unbiased
- Protection extends to those supporting the complaint process
- Separate reporting mechanism for retaliation complaints

Retaliation Includes:
× Demotion, transfer, or termination
× Negative performance reviews
× Exclusion from meetings or projects
× Hostile behavior or isolation
× Spreading rumors or defamation

Penalty: Retaliation is treated as a separate violation with independent disciplinary action.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

8. INTERIM RELIEF MEASURES

During Investigation:
The ICC may recommend interim measures to ensure complainant safety:

 Transfer of complainant or respondent to different team/location
 Granting leave to complainant (up to 3 months)
 Restraining order preventing direct contact
 Remote work arrangement
 Access to counseling services
 Flexible working hours

These measures are precautionary and do not imply guilt.

9. FALSE OR MALICIOUS COMPLAINTS

Policy on False Complaints:
While we encourage reporting genuine concerns, deliberately false or malicious complaints undermine the process and harm individuals.

Investigation:
If ICC finds evidence of malicious intent or false allegations:
- Complainant may face disciplinary action
- Same penalty as would have applied to respondent if guilty
- Does NOT apply to unproven complaints made in good faith

Protection:
Inability to substantiate a complaint is NOT the same as a false complaint. Good faith reporting is always protected.

13. EXTERNAL REMEDIES

Employees have the right to pursue external remedies:

Local Complaints Committee (LCC):
- Available in areas where company ICC doesn't exist
- Established by District Officer
- Same powers as ICC

Criminal Complaint:
- File FIR at police station for criminal offenses
- Company will cooperate with investigation
- No restriction on pursuing criminal remedies

Civil Suit:
- Sue for damages and compensation
- Can be pursued alongside or after ICC process

Time Bar: Criminal complaints should be filed within legal time limits (usually within 3 months for certain offenses).

14. REMOTE WORK AND VIRTUAL HARASSMENT

Policy Extension for Digital Workplace:

Virtual Sexual Harassment includes:
- Inappropriate comments during video calls
- Unwelcome personal messages on work platforms
- Sharing explicit content via email/chat
- Stalking on social media related to work
- Recording meetings without consent for improper purposes
- Zoom-bombing with sexual content

Same Process Applies:
All complaints of virtual harassment follow the same ICC process with appropriate investigation methods for digital evidence.
