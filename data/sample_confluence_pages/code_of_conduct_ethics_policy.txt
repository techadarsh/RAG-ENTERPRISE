## 1. Core Values & Principles

### 1.1 Our Core Values

**RESPECT**:
- Treat everyone with dignity, regardless of position or background
- Value diverse perspectives and experiences
- Listen actively and communicate respectfully
- Foster inclusive environment where all feel welcome

**INTEGRITY**:
- Be honest in all communications and actions
- Admit mistakes and take responsibility
- Keep commitments and promises
- Do the right thing even when no one is watching

**COLLABORATION**:
- Work together toward common goals
- Share knowledge and help teammates succeed
- Give credit where credit is due
- Resolve conflicts constructively

**EXCELLENCE**:
- Strive for high-quality work
- Continuously improve skills and processes
- Take pride in deliverables
- Hold self and others accountable

**INNOVATION**:
- Embrace new ideas and approaches
- Challenge status quo respectfully
- Learn from failures
- Experiment and take calculated risks

### 1.2 Ethical Decision-Making Framework

When facing ethical dilemmas, ask yourself:

1. **Is it legal?** Does it comply with laws and regulations?
2. **Is it ethical?** Does it align with our values?
3. **How would it look?** Would I be comfortable if this appeared in the news?
4. **Is it fair?** Would I want to be treated this way?
5. **Long-term impact?** What are the consequences?

**If uncertain**, consult with:
- Manager
- HR department
- Legal team
- Ethics hotline (anonymous)

## 2. Workplace Conduct Standards

### 2.1 Professional Behavior

**Communication Standards**:

✅ **DO**:
- Use respectful language in all communications
- Be clear, concise, and constructive
- Respond to emails/messages within 24 hours (work hours)
- Use appropriate channels (Slack for quick questions, email for formal)
- Proofread before sending

❌ **DON'T**:
- Use profanity, insults, or aggressive language
- Make personal attacks or belittling comments
- Send messages when angry (wait, cool down, then respond)
- Use all caps (perceived as shouting)
- Share confidential information in public channels

**Meeting Etiquette**:
- Arrive on time (5 minutes early is on time)
- Come prepared (read materials in advance)
- Mute when not speaking (video calls)
- Video on for internal meetings (unless bandwidth issues)
- No multitasking (close email, focus on discussion)
- Respect speaking time (don't monopolize)
- End on time (respect others' schedules)

**Punctuality & Attendance**:
- Arrive on time for work (within 15-minute grace period)
- Notify manager if running late (before start time)
- No more than 2 late arrivals per month
- Unexcused absences not tolerated (call/text before shift)
- If sick, notify manager ASAP (before 9 AM)

### 2.2 Teamwork & Collaboration

**Expected Behaviors**:
- Offer help to teammates proactively
- Share knowledge (documentation, mentoring)
- Give constructive feedback (focus on behavior, not person)
- Receive feedback gracefully (don't be defensive)
- Credit team contributions publicly
- Support team decisions even if you initially disagreed
- Resolve disagreements privately first

**Code Review Etiquette**:
- Review within 24 hours of assignment
- Be respectful and constructive ("Consider refactoring" vs "This is wrong")
- Explain reasoning behind suggestions
- Accept that reviewers may be right
- Approve when code meets standards (don't nitpick)

### 2.3 Accountability & Ownership

**Taking Responsibility**:
- Own your commitments (meet deadlines)
- If you'll miss a deadline, notify ASAP (at least 48 hours before)
- Admit mistakes promptly (no cover-ups)
- Propose solutions, not just problems
- Follow through on action items
- Update task status regularly (Jira/Asana)

**Quality Standards**:
- Test code before submitting PR
- Write clear documentation
- Don't cut corners to meet deadlines
- Ask for help when stuck (after trying for 2 hours)
- Review own work before asking others

## 3. Anti-Harassment & Discrimination

### 3.1 Zero Tolerance Policy

**Statement**:
Our company has a **zero-tolerance policy** for harassment and discrimination. We are committed to providing a workplace free from harassment, discrimination, and retaliation based on any protected characteristic.

**Protected Characteristics**:
- Race, color, ethnicity, national origin
- Sex, gender, gender identity, gender expression
- Sexual orientation
- Religion, creed
- Age (40 and older)
- Disability (physical or mental)
- Pregnancy, childbirth, related conditions
- Marital status
- Military or veteran status
- Genetic information
- Any other characteristic protected by law

### 3.2 Harassment Defined

**Sexual Harassment**:
Unwelcome sexual advances, requests for sexual favors, or other verbal/physical conduct of a sexual nature when:
- Submission is made explicitly or implicitly a term of employment
- Submission or rejection affects employment decisions
- Conduct unreasonably interferes with work performance or creates intimidating/hostile environment

**Examples**:
- Unwanted touching, hugging, kissing, or other physical contact
- Sexual jokes, comments, or innuendos
- Sharing sexually explicit images, videos, or materials
- Staring, leering, or making sexual gestures
- Asking about sexual history or preferences
- Unwanted romantic or sexual advances
- Retaliation for rejecting advances

**General Harassment**:
Unwelcome conduct based on protected characteristics that creates intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment.

**Examples**:
- Offensive jokes, slurs, epithets about protected characteristics
- Displaying offensive symbols, posters, or materials
- Mocking accents, clothing, or cultural practices
- Exclusion or isolation based on protected characteristics
- Physical assaults, threats, or intimidation
- Interference with work performance based on protected characteristics

### 3.3 Discrimination Defined

**Direct Discrimination**:
Treating someone less favorably because of a protected characteristic.

**Examples**:
- Not hiring qualified candidate due to race, gender, age
- Paying women less than men for same role
- Denying promotion due to pregnancy
- Terminating older employees first during layoffs
- Refusing reasonable accommodations for disabilities

**Indirect Discrimination**:
Policies or practices that appear neutral but disproportionately affect protected groups.

**Examples**:
- Requiring certain height/weight (excludes some ethnicities/genders)
- Mandatory evening meetings (affects parents disproportionately)
- Promoting only those who socialize after hours (excludes some groups)

### 3.4 Bullying & Intimidation

**Defined**:
Repeated, unreasonable behavior directed toward an employee that creates risk to health and safety.

**Examples**:
- Yelling, screaming, or aggressive behavior
- Public humiliation or belittling
- Spreading rumors or gossip
- Deliberately sabotaging work
- Exclusion from meetings or communications
- Excessive criticism or impossible demands
- Taking credit for others' work

**Note**: Bullying is prohibited even if not based on protected characteristic.

### 3.5 Reporting Harassment or Discrimination

**How to Report**:

1. **Direct Report to HR**:
   - Email: hr@company.com
   - Phone: (555) 123-4567
   - In-person: HR office (3rd floor)

2. **Report to Manager** (if comfortable):
   - Manager will escalate to HR
   - Manager must report within 24 hours

3. **Anonymous Hotline**:
   - 1-800-REPORT-IT (1-800-737-6784)
   - Available 24/7
   - Managed by third-party service
   - Fully confidential

4. **Online Portal**:
   - company.ethicspoint.com
   - Submit written complaint
   - Can remain anonymous

**What to Include**:
- Names of individuals involved
- Dates and times of incidents
- Location where incidents occurred
- Witnesses (if any)
- Detailed description of what happened
- Any supporting evidence (emails, messages, photos)

**Investigation Process**:
1. HR receives complaint (acknowledges within 2 business days)
2. Preliminary review (assess severity, immediate actions needed)
3. Investigation begins (within 5 business days)
4. Interviews conducted (complainant, accused, witnesses)
5. Evidence reviewed (emails, messages, video footage)
6. Findings documented (substantiated, unsubstantiated, or inconclusive)
7. Decision communicated (within 30 days of complaint)
8. Disciplinary action taken (if warranted)

**During Investigation**:
- Confidentiality maintained (only those who need to know)
- Both parties treated fairly and respectfully
- Interim measures may be taken (temporary reassignment, remote work)
- No retaliation against complainant or witnesses

**Possible Outcomes**:
- **Substantiated**: Harassment/discrimination occurred → Disciplinary action (warning to termination)
- **Unsubstantiated**: Insufficient evidence → No action, possibly mediation or training
- **Inconclusive**: Cannot determine → Monitoring, possibly additional training

### 3.6 Bystander Intervention

**Responsibility**:
If you witness harassment or discrimination, you have a responsibility to act.

**How to Intervene**:
1. **Direct**: "That comment is inappropriate. Please stop."
2. **Distract**: Change the subject or redirect conversation
3. **Delegate**: Report to manager or HR if uncomfortable intervening
4. **Support Victim**: Check in privately, offer support, encourage them to report

**Not Intervening**:
Witnesses who fail to report serious misconduct may face disciplinary action.

## 4. Conflicts of Interest

### 4.1 Definition

A **conflict of interest** occurs when personal interests interfere, or appear to interfere, with company interests.

**Key Principle**: Always act in the company's best interest, not personal gain.

### 4.2 Common Conflicts

**Outside Employment**:
- Working for competitor (prohibited)
- Working for customer or vendor (must disclose)
- Freelancing that uses company resources (prohibited)
- Side business that competes with company (prohibited)

**Financial Interests**:
- Owning significant stock in competitor, customer, or vendor (must disclose if >10%)
- Investing in companies you make business decisions about (prohibited)
- Personal loans from customers or vendors (prohibited)

**Personal Relationships**:
- Hiring, supervising, or evaluating family member or romantic partner (must disclose, reassignment may be required)
- Awarding contracts to companies owned by family/friends (must disclose, recuse from decision)
- Dating within reporting line (prohibited - one party must transfer)

**Gifts & Entertainment**:
- Accepting gifts >$100 from vendors (must report)
- Lavish entertainment from vendors (must report)
- Cash gifts from any business partner (prohibited)

**Board Positions**:
- Serving on board of competitor (prohibited)
- Serving on board of customer/vendor (must disclose and get approval)

### 4.3 Disclosure Requirements

**When to Disclose**:
- When conflict arises or potentially could arise
- Immediately upon awareness
- Don't wait for annual review

**How to Disclose**:
1. Email hr-conflicts@company.com with subject "Conflict of Interest Disclosure"
2. Include:
   - Nature of conflict
   - Parties involved
   - Financial interests (if applicable)
   - Proposed resolution
3. HR reviews and responds within 10 business days
4. May require recusal, divestment, or reassignment

**Annual Disclosure**:
- All employees complete annual conflict of interest questionnaire (January)
- Disclose any new conflicts that arose during year
- Update on previously disclosed conflicts

### 4.4 Managing Conflicts

**Acceptable Management**:
- Recuse yourself from decisions involving conflict
- Assign decision to another manager
- Create firewall (no involvement in relevant matters)
- Disclose to all parties and get written approval

**Unacceptable**:
- Hiding the conflict
- Proceeding without disclosure
- Using inside information for personal gain
- Letting personal interests influence business decisions

**Example Scenario**:
```
SITUATION: Your brother-in-law's company submits bid for company contract. You're on selection committee.

✅ CORRECT ACTION:
1. Immediately disclose relationship to manager and HR
2. Recuse yourself from selection committee
3. Don't discuss bid with brother-in-law
4. Document recusal in meeting notes

❌ INCORRECT ACTION:
1. Participate in selection without disclosure
2. Share insider information with brother-in-law
3. Advocate for his company's bid
```

## 5. Confidentiality & Data Protection

### 5.1 Confidential Information

**Defined**:
Any non-public information that could harm company or provide advantage to competitors if disclosed.

**Types of Confidential Information**:
- **Trade Secrets**: Algorithms, source code, technical designs, formulas
- **Business Information**: Strategies, financial data, pricing, customer lists, contract terms
- **Employee Information**: Salaries, performance reviews, medical records, social security numbers
- **Customer Data**: Contact info, purchase history, payment details, PII (Personally Identifiable Information)
- **Proprietary Data**: Research, product roadmaps, marketing plans, competitive analysis

### 5.2 Handling Confidential Information

**Protection Requirements**:
- ✅ Store on encrypted company devices only
- ✅ Use password-protected files (minimum 12 characters)
- ✅ Share only with those who need to know (least privilege)
- ✅ Mark documents as "Confidential" or "Internal"
- ✅ Use secure channels (company email, Slack DMs, not SMS)
- ✅ Shred paper documents (don't throw in trash)
- ✅ Lock screen when away from desk
- ✅ Use VPN when accessing from public networks

**Prohibited Actions**:
- ❌ Discussing confidential matters in public places (elevators, cafes, transportation)
- ❌ Sending company data to personal email
- ❌ Storing customer data on personal devices
- ❌ Sharing login credentials
- ❌ Taking confidential documents home (unless necessary and approved)
- ❌ Posting confidential information on social media
- ❌ Discussing confidential matters with family/friends

### 5.3 Data Breach Response

**If You Suspect Data Breach**:
1. **Immediately** notify security@company.com (within 1 hour)
2. Do NOT investigate on your own (may destroy evidence)
3. Do NOT discuss with anyone except security team
4. Preserve evidence (don't delete logs, emails, etc.)

**What is a Data Breach?**:
- Unauthorized access to customer data
- Accidental email to wrong recipient containing PII
- Lost/stolen device with company data
- Ransomware or malware infection
- Suspicious account activity

**Timeline**:
- Report within **1 hour** of discovery
- Security team investigates within **2 hours**
- Affected parties notified within **72 hours** (GDPR requirement)
- Post-incident review within **1 week**

### 5.4 Clean Desk Policy

**Requirements**:
- Lock confidential documents in drawer or cabinet when not in use
- Don't leave documents on printer or in meeting rooms
- Clear whiteboards after meetings with confidential info
- Dispose of drafts and notes securely (shred)
- Log out of computer when leaving desk for >5 minutes

### 5.5 Confidentiality After Employment

**Obligations Continue**:
- NDA survives employment termination (perpetual)
- Cannot disclose confidential information to new employer
- Must return all company property (laptop, documents, access badges)
- Cannot solicit company customers or employees for 12 months (non-solicitation)
- Cannot compete directly for 12 months (non-compete, if signed)

**Exit Interview**:
- Reminder of confidentiality obligations
- Return of all company property
- Deactivation of accounts and access
- Final paycheck includes unused PTO

## 6. Intellectual Property Rights

### 6.1 Ownership of Work Product

**Company Owns**:
All work created during employment belongs to company:
- Source code written for company projects
- Documentation, technical designs, architecture diagrams
- Inventions, discoveries, improvements related to company business
- Patents, copyrights, trademarks, trade secrets
- Presentations, blog posts, articles about company work

**Applies to**:
- Work done during work hours
- Work done using company resources (laptop, software, office)
- Work related to company's business (even if done at home after hours)

**Examples**:
- ✅ Company Owns: Developing new feature for company's product (even if idea came on weekend)
- ✅ Company Owns: Writing internal tool to automate task (even if during lunch break)
- ❌ Company Does NOT Own: Personal blog about gardening (unrelated to work)
- ❌ Company Does NOT Own: Novel written on own time without company resources

### 6.2 Personal Projects

**Allowed If**:
1. Completely unrelated to company's business
2. Done entirely outside work hours
3. No use of company resources (devices, software, data, office space)
4. Doesn't compete with company
5. Doesn't use company confidential information

**Disclosure Recommended**:
While not required, it's wise to disclose personal projects to manager/HR to avoid future disputes.

**Disclosure Form**:
```
Personal Project Disclosure Form
- Project name and description
- How it relates (or doesn't) to company business
- Resources used (own laptop, own time)
- Intent (hobby, side income, future startup)
- Signature and date
```

**Approval Process**:
- Submit disclosure form to HR
- HR reviews (5-10 business days)
- Approved, conditional approval, or denied
- Conditions might include: regular updates, restrictions on use

### 6.3 Open Source Contributions

**Company Policy**:
Employees may contribute to open source projects with approval.

**Approval Required For**:
- Contributing company code to open source
- Open sourcing internal tools
- Using company time for contributions (even if related to work)

**No Approval Needed For**:
- Personal contributions on own time (unrelated to company work)
- Bug fixes to libraries used by company (encouraged!)
- Answering questions on Stack Overflow

**How to Get Approval**:
1. Submit request via opensource@company.com
2. Include: Project name, license, what you'll contribute, why it benefits company
3. Legal reviews (5-10 business days)
4. Approval granted or denied

### 6.4 Using Third-Party IP

**Software Licenses**:
Before using third-party libraries/code, verify license compatibility:
- ✅ **Permissive Licenses** (MIT, Apache 2.0, BSD): Generally okay, minimal restrictions
- ⚠️ **Weak Copyleft** (LGPL, MPL): Okay if used as library (not modified)
- ❌ **Strong Copyleft** (GPL, AGPL): Prohibited for proprietary software (requires legal review)

**License Review Process**:
1. Check license file in repository
2. If permissive → Okay to use (document in dependencies)
3. If copyleft or uncertain → Submit to legal@company.com for review
4. Wait for approval before using

**Prohibited Actions**:
- Copying code from Stack Overflow without attribution (plagiarism)
- Using cracked/pirated software
- Violating license terms (e.g., removing attribution)

## 7. Financial Integrity & Fraud Prevention

### 7.1 Accurate Record Keeping

**Requirements**:
- All business records must be accurate, complete, and timely
- Expense reports must reflect actual expenses
- Time tracking must be honest (no rounding up)
- Financial reports must comply with GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles)

**Examples of Violations**:
- Inflating expenses or submitting false receipts
- Logging time not actually worked
- Falsifying sales numbers or revenue
- Hiding losses or liabilities
- Backdating documents

**Consequences**:
- Immediate termination
- Criminal charges (fraud)
- Repayment of fraudulent amounts
- Permanent disqualification from rehire

### 7.2 Expense Reimbursement

**Eligible Expenses**:
- Business travel (flights, hotels, rental cars)
- Client meals and entertainment
- Conference registrations
- Professional development (books, courses)
- Home office equipment (if remote)

**Limits**:
- Meals: $50/day domestic, $75/day international
- Hotels: Up to $250/night (standard business hotels)
- Flights: Economy class (business class if >6 hours with approval)
- Rental cars: Standard/midsize (no luxury vehicles)

**Receipt Requirements**:
- Required for expenses >$25
- Must be itemized (not just credit card receipt)
- Submit within 30 days of expense
- Include business purpose and attendees (for meals)

**Prohibited Expenses**:
- Personal meals (not with clients)
- Alcoholic beverages (unless client meal)
- Entertainment (movies, concerts) unless client-related
- Spouse/family travel
- First-class flights without approval
- Luxury hotels or rental cars

**Submitting Expenses**:
1. Log into expense system (Expensify)
2. Upload receipts (photo or PDF)
3. Categorize expense (travel, meals, etc.)
4. Add business purpose
5. Submit for approval
6. Manager approves within 5 days
7. Reimbursed in next paycheck

### 7.3 Fraud Prevention

**Definition of Fraud**:
Intentional deception for personal gain or to harm company.

**Types of Fraud**:
- **Expense Fraud**: Inflated or fake expense reports
- **Time Fraud**: Logging hours not worked
- **Procurement Fraud**: Kickbacks from vendors, fake invoices
- **Financial Fraud**: Embezzlement, misappropriation of funds
- **Payroll Fraud**: Ghost employees, overstating hours

**Red Flags**:
- Living beyond means (unexplained wealth)
- Reluctance to take vacation (fear of discovery)
- Control freak (won't delegate or share duties)
- Unusual vendor relationships (always chooses same vendor)
- Missing documents or records

**Reporting Suspected Fraud**:
- Report to fraud@company.com or anonymous hotline
- Investigation by internal audit team
- Protection for whistleblowers (non-retaliation)
- Confidentiality maintained

### 7.4 Anti-Money Laundering & Sanctions

**Prohibited Activities**:
- Accepting cash payments >$10,000
- Doing business with sanctioned countries (North Korea, Iran, Syria, etc.)
- Transactions with terrorists or criminal organizations
- Structuring payments to avoid reporting (smurfing)

**Know Your Customer (KYC)**:
- Verify customer identity before onboarding
- Screen against sanctions lists (OFAC)
- Monitor transactions for suspicious activity
- Report suspicious activity to compliance (suspicious activity report - SAR)

## 8. Gifts, Entertainment & Bribery

### 8.1 Gifts & Entertainment Policy

**Receiving Gifts**:

```
VALUE          | ACTION REQUIRED
$0-$25         | Accept, no reporting
$26-$100       | Accept, report to manager
$101-$250      | Report to manager, approval required
>$250          | Decline or seek VP approval
Cash (any amt) | Decline (always prohibited)
```

**Giving Gifts**:
- Maximum $100 per recipient per year
- Must have legitimate business purpose
- No cash or cash equivalents
- Pre-approval for gifts >$50

**Entertainment**:
- Meals, sporting events, conferences allowed if reasonable
- Must include business discussion
- Should not create obligation or appearance of impropriety
- Lavish entertainment (>$500) requires VP approval

**Prohibited**:
- Cash or cash equivalents (gift cards, checks, stock)
- Gifts to government officials (any amount, see FCPA)
- Gifts during contract negotiations
- Gifts given with expectation of favor

**Examples**:

✅ **Acceptable**:
- Vendor sends $50 fruit basket during holidays → Accept, report to manager
- Client invites you to baseball game ($75 tickets) → Accept, must discuss business
- Vendor offers coffee mug with company logo ($10) → Accept, no reporting

❌ **Not Acceptable**:
- Vendor offers $500 gift card for choosing their product → Decline, report to compliance
- Government official expects expensive meal for contract approval → Decline, report to legal
- Vendor offers free vacation ($5,000 value) → Decline immediately

### 8.2 Anti-Bribery & Corruption (FCPA Compliance)

**Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA)**:
US law prohibiting bribing foreign government officials to obtain or retain business.

**Prohibited**:
- Paying, offering, or promising anything of value to foreign government official
- Using third-party (agent, consultant) to make such payments
- "Facilitating payments" to expedite routine government actions (even if small)

**Who is a "Government Official"?**:
- Elected officials and civil servants
- Employees of state-owned enterprises (SOEs)
- Political party officials
- Employees of public international organizations (UN, World Bank)

**Examples of Violations**:
- Paying customs official to expedite shipment
- Offering gift to procurement officer to win contract
- Hiring government official's relative as "consultant" (no real work)
- Donating to charity controlled by government official (to gain favor)

**Consequences**:
- Fines up to $2 million (company), $250,000 (individual)
- Prison up to 5 years
- Debarment from government contracts
- Reputational damage

**If Approached for Bribe**:
1. Refuse politely ("Company policy prohibits")
2. Document conversation (date, time, person, request)
3. Report immediately to legal@company.com
4. Do NOT make payment (even if it means losing deal)

### 8.3 Due Diligence on Third Parties

**Before Hiring Agent/Consultant/Vendor**:
- Conduct background check
- Review contracts for red flags (unusual commissions, vague services)
- Include anti-bribery clauses in contracts
- Monitor payments and activities

**Red Flags**:
- Request for cash payments
- Payments to offshore accounts
- Unusual payment structures (large upfront fees)
- Family relationship with government official
- Reputation for corruption

## 9. Social Media & Public Communications

### 9.1 Personal Social Media Use

**Guidelines**:
- ✅ You may discuss work in general terms ("Excited about new project!")
- ✅ You may share company's public content (retweet, share blog posts)
- ✅ You may list company as employer on LinkedIn
- ❌ Do NOT share confidential information (code, product roadmaps, financials)
- ❌ Do NOT speak on behalf of company (unless authorized)
- ❌ Do NOT post derogatory comments about company, colleagues, or customers
- ❌ Do NOT post content that violates code of conduct (harassment, discrimination)

**Disclaimer Required**:
If discussing work-related topics, include disclaimer:
> "Opinions are my own and do not represent my employer."

**Best Practices**:
- Think before posting (would you be okay with manager/customer seeing it?)
- Be respectful and professional
- Correct mistakes promptly
- Don't engage in online arguments about company
- Consider if post could damage company reputation

**Examples**:

✅ **Acceptable**:
- "Proud of what we shipped today! Great teamwork. #productlaunch"
- Sharing company's blog post about new feature
- LinkedIn post: "Learned a lot about Docker this week"

❌ **Not Acceptable**:
- Posting screenshot of internal Slack conversation
- Tweeting about company's upcoming acquisition (confidential)
- Complaining about manager on Facebook
- Sharing customer data or PII

### 9.2 Speaking on Behalf of Company

**Authorization Required**:
Only authorized spokespeople may speak to media or represent company publicly.

**Authorized Spokespeople**:
- CEO
- VP of Marketing
- PR team

**Media Inquiries**:
If contacted by media:
1. Do NOT answer questions
2. Say: "I'll connect you with our communications team"
3. Forward inquiry to pr@company.com immediately
4. Include: Reporter name, outlet, topic, deadline

**Speaking at Conferences**:
- Must get approval from manager and marketing
- Company logo on slides requires brand team approval
- Don't share confidential information
- Focus on industry trends, technical topics (not company strategy)

### 9.3 Internal Communications

**Email & Slack**:
- Be professional (company can monitor all communications)
- No expectation of privacy on company systems
- Avoid gossip, rumors, or negative comments about colleagues
- Use appropriate channels (public vs. private)
- Follow retention policies (don't delete emails related to legal matters)

**Meeting Communications**:
- Respect confidentiality of internal discussions
- Don't record meetings without permission
- Mark sensitive documents as "Internal" or "Confidential"

## 10. Health, Safety & Substance Abuse

### 10.1 Workplace Safety

**Employee Responsibilities**:
- Follow safety procedures and guidelines
- Use equipment properly (no shortcuts)
- Report hazards immediately (facilities@company.com)
- Participate in fire drills and safety training
- Wear required safety equipment (if applicable)

**Ergonomics**:
- Set up workstation properly (monitor at eye level, chair adjusted)
- Take breaks every hour (stand, stretch, rest eyes)
- Request ergonomic assessment if experiencing discomfort

**Emergency Procedures**:
- Know emergency exits (at least 2 from your location)
- Know assembly point (parking lot across street)
- Follow evacuation instructions (don't collect belongings)
- Don't re-enter building until all-clear given

**Reporting Incidents**:
- Report all injuries, no matter how minor
- Report near-misses (helps prevent future incidents)
- Fill out incident report within 24 hours
- Seek medical attention if needed (workers' comp covers)

### 10.2 Substance Abuse Policy

**Prohibited Substances**:
- Illegal drugs (marijuana, cocaine, etc.) - even if legal in state
- Alcohol during work hours (except company-sanctioned events)
- Prescription drugs that impair performance (if not prescribed to you)

**Testing**:
- Pre-employment drug test (all candidates)
- Random testing (safety-sensitive positions only)
- Post-accident testing (if reasonable suspicion)
- Reasonable suspicion testing (if behavior indicates impairment)

**Signs of Impairment**:
- Slurred speech, bloodshot eyes, odor of alcohol
- Erratic behavior, mood swings
- Decreased coordination, slow reaction time
- Frequent absences or tardiness

**Consequences**:
- First offense: Mandatory EAP enrollment, suspension pending completion
- Second offense: Final warning, continued EAP participation
- Third offense: Termination

**Exceptions**:
- Company events where alcohol served (happy hours, holiday party) - okay in moderation
- Client dinners - okay if reasonable (1-2 drinks)
- Prescription medications legally prescribed to you - allowed, but notify manager if it may impair performance

### 10.3 Smoking Policy

**Designated Areas Only**:
- Smoking (including vaping) allowed only in designated outdoor areas
- Minimum 25 feet from building entrances
- Must dispose of cigarette butts properly

**Prohibited**:
- Smoking inside building (anywhere)
- Smoking in company vehicles
- Smoking within 25 feet of doors/windows

**Smoking Cessation Support**:
- EAP counseling available
- Nicotine replacement therapy (NRT) covered by health insurance
- Smoking cessation programs reimbursed

## 11. Reporting Violations & Non-Retaliation

### 11.1 Duty to Report

**Mandatory Reporting**:
All employees have a duty to report:
- Violations of this Code of Conduct
- Illegal or unethical behavior
- Harassment or discrimination
- Safety hazards
- Fraud or financial misconduct
- Conflicts of interest
- Data breaches or security incidents

**Failure to Report**:
- Disciplinary action for failing to report known violations
- Especially for managers (higher duty to report)

### 11.2 Reporting Channels

**Option 1: Direct Manager**:
- Most issues resolved at this level
- Manager required to escalate serious issues to HR

**Option 2: HR Department**:
- hr@company.com
- (555) 123-4567
- In-person (3rd floor)

**Option 3: Anonymous Hotline**:
- 1-800-REPORT-IT (1-800-737-6784)
- Available 24/7
- Managed by third-party (EthicsPoint)
- Fully anonymous (not tracked or recorded)
- Can follow up on case with provided reference number

**Option 4: Online Portal**:
- company.ethicspoint.com
- Submit written report
- Can attach supporting documents
- Can remain anonymous or provide contact info

**Option 5: Legal Department**:
- For legal/compliance matters
- legal@company.com

**Option 6: Executive Team**:
- If issue involves direct manager or HR
- VP contact info available on intranet

### 11.3 Good Faith Reporting

**Good Faith** means:
- You honestly believe a violation occurred
- You report based on facts, not rumors
- You're not making false accusations intentionally

**Protected Even If Mistaken**:
- If you report in good faith but investigation finds no violation, you're still protected
- No retaliation for good faith reports

**Bad Faith Reporting**:
- Knowingly making false accusations
- Reporting to harm someone (malicious)
- Consequences: Disciplinary action up to termination

### 11.4 Non-Retaliation Policy

**Strict Prohibition**:
Company strictly prohibits retaliation against anyone who:
- Reports violation in good faith
- Participates in investigation
- Refuses to participate in wrongdoing
- Exercises legal rights (files workers' comp claim, takes FMLA leave)

**Retaliation Defined**:
Any adverse action taken because of reporting or participating in investigation:
- Termination, demotion, suspension
- Reduced pay or hours
- Negative performance review
- Exclusion from meetings or projects
- Social isolation or hostility
- Threats or intimidation

**Zero Tolerance**:
- Retaliation is grounds for immediate termination
- Applies to all employees (including executives)
- Applies even if original complaint was unsubstantiated

**Reporting Retaliation**:
- Report to HR immediately
- Investigation within 5 business days
- Interim protective measures (reassignment, remote work)

### 11.5 Investigation Process

**Timeline**:
- Acknowledgment within 2 business days
- Investigation begins within 5 business days
- Completion within 30 days (may extend for complex cases)
- Follow-up with reporter within 5 days of conclusion

**Process**:
1. HR receives report
2. Preliminary assessment (severity, urgency)
3. Investigation team assigned (HR + legal if needed)
4. Interviews conducted (complainant, accused, witnesses)
5. Evidence reviewed (emails, documents, video)
6. Findings documented
7. Decision made (substantiated, unsubstantiated, inconclusive)
8. Disciplinary action taken (if warranted)
9. Follow-up and monitoring

**Confidentiality**:
- Only those who need to know are informed
- Investigators bound by confidentiality
- Complainant identity protected (unless disclosure necessary for investigation)

**Fair Process**:
- Both parties treated with respect
- Accused given opportunity to respond
- Witnesses interviewed impartially
- Decision based on evidence, not bias

## 12. Enforcement & Disciplinary Actions

### 12.1 Disciplinary Process

**Progressive Discipline**:
For most violations, we use progressive discipline:

1. **Verbal Warning**:
   - Informal discussion with manager
   - Explain violation and expected behavior
   - Documented in manager's notes

2. **Written Warning**:
   - Formal written notice placed in personnel file
   - Detailed description of violation
   - Consequences if behavior continues
   - Employee signs acknowledgment

3. **Suspension**:
   - Unpaid suspension (1-5 days)
   - Time to reflect on behavior
   - Final chance to correct course

4. **Termination**:
   - Employment ended
   - Exit interview
   - Final paycheck includes unused PTO (if eligible)

**Exceptions (Immediate Termination)**:
Some violations are so serious they result in immediate termination:
- Violence or threats
- Theft or fraud
- Serious harassment or discrimination
- Illegal activity (drug dealing, embezzlement)
- Gross negligence causing harm
- Disclosure of trade secrets

### 12.2 Factors Considered

**Aggravating Factors** (increase severity):
- Intent (deliberate vs. accidental)
- Harm caused (financial loss, reputational damage)
- Prior warnings or violations
- Position (managers held to higher standard)
- Attempted cover-up

**Mitigating Factors** (decrease severity):
- First offense
- Self-reported
- Cooperation in investigation
- Genuine remorse
- Steps taken to prevent recurrence

### 12.3 Appeals

**Right to Appeal**:
Employee may appeal disciplinary decision within 10 business days.

**Process**:
1. Submit written appeal to VP of HR
2. Include: Why decision was wrong, new evidence, mitigating factors
3. VP reviews case independently
4. Decision within 15 business days
5. VP decision is final (no further appeals)

**Grounds for Appeal**:
- New evidence not available during investigation
- Procedural errors in investigation
- Punishment disproportionate to violation
- Bias or conflict of interest in investigation

### 12.4 Recordkeeping

**Documentation**:
- All disciplinary actions documented in personnel file
- Includes: Date, violation, evidence, decision, employee response
- Retained for 7 years after employment ends

**Confidentiality**:
- Access restricted (HR, direct manager, executives)
- Not disclosed to other employees
- May be disclosed if legally required (subpoena, audit)
