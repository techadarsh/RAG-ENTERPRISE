## 1. Remote Work Options & Eligibility

### 1.1 Work Arrangement Types

**Fully Remote (100% Remote)**:
- Work from home or approved location 100% of time
- No assigned office desk
- Must attend quarterly in-person gatherings (company-paid travel)
- Available for most engineering, product, and some support roles

**Hybrid (2-3 Days Office)**:
- Office days: Tuesday & Wednesday (core collaboration days) + 1 flexible day
- Remote days: Monday, Thursday, Friday (choose 2)
- Assigned desk or hot-desking (depending on office space)
- Default arrangement for most roles

**Flexible In-Office**:
- Primarily office-based but can work remotely occasionally
- Remote work requires manager approval (1-2 days/week)
- Assigned permanent desk
- Required for some roles (IT support, facilities, lab equipment)

**Fully In-Office**:
- 5 days per week in office
- Required for specific positions (receptionist, lab technicians)
- No regular remote work

### 1.2 Eligibility Criteria

**Eligible for Remote/Hybrid**:
-  Completed 90-day probationary period
-  "Meets Expectations" or higher performance rating
-  Role can be performed remotely (manager determination)
-  No active performance improvement plan (PIP)
-  Demonstrated self-motivation and time management
-  Reliable internet and home office setup

**Ineligible**:
-  Probationary employees (first 90 days)
-  Roles requiring physical presence (lab work, reception, facilities)
-  Performance issues (rating <3)
-  Inadequate home office setup
-  Not meeting productivity/collaboration expectations

**Example Eligible Roles**:
- Software engineers
- Data scientists
- Product managers
- Designers
- Marketing team
- Customer success (phone-based)
- Finance/accounting
- HR (most positions)

**Example Ineligible Roles**:
- Receptionist (needs physical presence)
- Lab technicians (equipment access)
- Facilities management
- IT hardware support (hands-on)
- Some managerial roles (team-building)

### 1.3 Application Process

**How to Request**:
1. Discuss with manager (gauge receptiveness)
2. Submit formal request via HR portal (Workday)
3. Include:
   - Desired arrangement (fully remote, hybrid, flexible)
   - Start date
   - Home office details (internet speed, workspace)
   - Communication plan (how you'll stay connected)
   - Productivity measures (how success will be tracked)

**Approval Process**:
- Manager reviews (considers role, performance, team needs)
- HR reviews (verifies eligibility)
- Decision within 10 business days
- If approved: 30-day trial period

**Trial Period (30 Days)**:
- Test arrangement for fit
- Manager assesses: Productivity, communication, collaboration
- Check-in at 2 weeks and 4 weeks
- Outcomes:
  - **Success**: Arrangement becomes permanent
  - **Issues**: Modify arrangement or revert to office
  - **Failure**: Return to office full-time

**Denial Appeals**:
- If denied, can appeal to HR within 10 days
- VP of HR reviews
- Decision final

### 1.4 Changes to Arrangement

**Employee-Initiated Changes**:
- Can request change every 6 months
- Follow same application process
- Business needs permitting

**Company-Initiated Changes**:
- Company may change arrangement with 30 days' notice
- Reasons: Business needs, performance issues, role changes
- If moving to more office days, assist with transition

**Temporary Changes**:
- Travel: Can work remotely temporarily (notify manager)
- Office renovation: May work remotely temporarily
- Personal circumstances: Discuss with manager (case-by-case)

## 2. Home Office Requirements

### 2.1 Workspace Requirements

**Dedicated Workspace**:
-  Separate room or dedicated area (not kitchen table long-term)
-  Quiet environment (minimal background noise for calls)
-  Adequate lighting (natural + task lighting)
-  Ergonomic setup (proper desk height, chair support)
-  Professional background for video calls
-  Not shared space where frequent interruptions occur

**Photos Required**:
- Submit 2 photos of workspace during application
- Shows: Desk setup, lighting, background for video calls
- HR reviews for adequacy

**Examples**:

 **Acceptable Workspace**:
- Spare bedroom converted to office
- Finished basement with desk area
- Corner of living room with room divider (quiet, professional)

 **Not Acceptable Workspace**:
- Kitchen table (unless dedicated office not possible + approved)
- Bedroom with bed visible in video background
- Hallway or high-traffic area
- Unfinished basement (poor lighting, unprofessional)

### 2.2 Internet Requirements

**Minimum Speeds**:
- **Download**: 50 Mbps (minimum), 100 Mbps (recommended)
- **Upload**: 10 Mbps (minimum), 25 Mbps (recommended)
- **Latency**: <50ms (for VPN, video calls)

**Testing**:
- Test at www.speedtest.net
- Submit screenshot during application
- Test during typical work hours (not 2 AM)

**Insufficient Internet**:
- If below minimum, consider:
  - Upgrading plan (company may reimburse)
  - Using mobile hotspot as backup
  - Working from coworking space (reimbursed)
  - Hybrid arrangement (office 3-4 days)

**Backup Internet**:
- Recommended: Mobile hotspot as backup (for outages)
- Company provides: Backup 4G/5G dongle for critical roles (IT, on-call engineers)

### 2.3 Safety & Environment

**Home Office Safety**:
- Free from hazards (no tripping hazards, proper electrical setup)
- Smoke detector in room
- Proper ventilation
- Emergency exit accessible
- First aid kit nearby (recommended)

**Environmental Considerations**:
- Temperature controlled (68-76°F ideal)
- Adequate humidity (40-60%)
- No mold, allergens, or air quality issues
- Proper electrical outlets (no overloaded power strips)

**Inspections**:
- No physical home inspections (privacy respected)
- Virtual inspection if ergonomic assessment requested
- Employee attests workspace meets requirements

## 4. Communication & Collaboration

### 4.1 Communication Channels

**Primary Tools**:

**Slack** (Instant Messaging):
- Real-time communication
- Channels: #engineering, #general, #random, #help-desk
- Direct messages for quick questions
- Response expectation: Within 1 hour during work hours
- Status: Update when away (lunch, meeting, busy)

**Email** (Asynchronous):
- Formal communications
- External communications (customers, vendors)
- Documentation (proposals, contracts)
- Response expectation: Within 24 hours (work days)

**Zoom** (Video Conferencing):
- Meetings (1-on-1s, team meetings, all-hands)
- Video ON for internal meetings (builds rapport)
- Video optional for large meetings (>20 people)
- Screen sharing for presentations
- Recording: Ask permission before recording

**GitHub** (Code Collaboration):
- Pull requests (code reviews)
- Issues (bug tracking, feature requests)
- Discussions (technical discussions)
- Response expectation: PR reviews within 24 hours

**Jira** (Project Management):
- Task tracking
- Sprint planning
- Status updates (In Progress, In Review, Done)
- Update tasks daily

**Confluence** (Documentation):
- Knowledge base
- Project documentation
- Meeting notes
- Runbooks

### 4.2 Communication Best Practices

**Response Times**:

```
CHANNEL          | URGENCY  | EXPECTED RESPONSE
-----------------|----------|------------------------------------------
Slack DM         | High     | Within 1 hour (work hours)
Slack channel    | Medium   | Within 2-4 hours (work hours)
Email            | Low      | Within 24 hours (work days)
Jira comment     | Low      | Within 1-2 business days
Phone call       | Urgent   | Immediate (if available)
```

**Overcommunication Encouraged**:
Remote work requires MORE communication than office:
- Update team on progress (daily standup)
- Share blockers early (don't wait till deadline)
- Ask questions proactively (don't assume)
- Document decisions (don't rely on verbal)

**Asynchronous Communication**:
- Default to async (respects time zones, focus time)
- Don't expect immediate responses
- Provide context (don't just say "hi")
- Use threads (keep conversations organized)

**Example Good Messages**:

 **Good**:
> "Hey @john, I'm working on the authentication feature and need your input on the API design. I've drafted a proposal here: [link]. Can you review by EOD tomorrow? No rush if you're busy, just ping me when you get to it. Thanks!"

 **Bad**:
> "Hi"
> (waits for response)
> "Are you there?"
> "I need help"
> (person finally responds 2 hours later)
> "Never mind, figured it out"

### 4.3 Meetings & Video Calls

**Video Call Etiquette**:

**Before Meeting**:
- Test audio/video (join 2 minutes early)
- Check background (tidy, professional)
- Good lighting (face lit, not backlit)
- Mute notifications (silence phone, close email)

**During Meeting**:
-  Video ON (builds rapport, shows engagement)
-  Mute when not speaking (reduces background noise)
-  Look at camera when speaking (eye contact)
-  Use chat for questions (don't interrupt)
-  Raise hand feature (for turn-taking)
-  Multitasking (no email, coding, browsing)
-  Eating (unless explicitly a lunch meeting)
-  Lying in bed (unprofessional)

**After Meeting**:
- Send summary (action items, decisions, next steps)
- Update Jira/Confluence (document outcomes)
- Follow up on commitments

**Meeting Scheduling**:
- Use calendar (Google Calendar)
- Include: Agenda, Zoom link, meeting goals
- Schedule during core hours (10 AM - 4 PM)
- Respect time zones (use Calendly for async scheduling)
- Include "no-meeting" blocks (focus time)

**Meeting Types**:

**Daily Standup** (15 min):
- What I did yesterday
- What I'm doing today
- Any blockers
- Video optional, async acceptable (post in Slack)

**Weekly 1-on-1** (30 min):
- Career development
- Feedback (both directions)
- Questions, concerns
- Video required (builds relationship)

**Sprint Planning** (1-2 hours):
- Review last sprint
- Plan next sprint
- Estimate tasks
- Video required

**All-Hands** (1 hour monthly):
- Company updates
- Q&A with leadership
- Team recognition
- Video required (or attend in-person)

### 4.4 Collaboration Tools

**Screen Sharing**:
- Use for: Debugging, presentations, demos
- Optimize: Close unnecessary tabs/apps
- Annotate: Use Zoom's annotation tools

**Virtual Whiteboarding**:
- Tools: Miro, Mural, Jamboard
- Use for: Brainstorming, diagramming, planning
- Async collaboration (team can contribute anytime)

**Pair Programming**:
- Tools: VS Code Live Share, Tuple, Screen
- Real-time collaboration on code
- Great for onboarding, debugging, knowledge sharing

**Document Collaboration**:
- Google Docs (real-time editing)
- Suggesting mode (for reviews)
- Comment for questions (resolved when addressed)

## 5. Work Hours & Availability

### 5.1 Core Hours

**Core Hours**: 10:00 AM - 4:00 PM (employee's local time)
- Must be available during core hours (responsive on Slack, available for meetings)
- All meetings scheduled during core hours (exceptions with advance notice)

**Flexible Hours Outside Core**:
- Start anytime: 7:00 AM - 10:00 AM
- End anytime: 4:00 PM - 8:00 PM
- Total: 40 hours per week

**Example Schedules**:

```
EARLY BIRD:
7:00 AM - 4:00 PM (includes 1-hour lunch)

STANDARD:
9:00 AM - 5:00 PM (includes 1-hour lunch)

NIGHT OWL:
10:00 AM - 7:00 PM (includes 1-hour lunch)

PARENT-FRIENDLY (broken day):
8:00 AM - 12:00 PM (work)
12:00 PM - 3:00 PM (school pickup, errands)
3:00 PM - 7:00 PM (work)
```

### 5.2 Time Tracking

**Exempt Employees** (salaried):
- No time tracking required (honor system)
- Expected to work ~40-45 hours/week
- Occasionally more during crunch times (compensated with comp time)

**Non-Exempt Employees** (hourly):
- Must track time via system (Clockify, Harvest)
- Clock in/out for shifts
- Log breaks (lunch, rest breaks)
- Overtime pre-approved (time-and-a-half for >40 hours/week)

**Breaks**:
- Lunch: 30-60 minutes (unpaid if >30 min)
- Rest breaks: 15 minutes every 4 hours (paid)
- No need to announce breaks (just update Slack status)

### 5.3 Availability Indicators

**Slack Status**:
Update status to indicate availability:
-  Available (default during work hours)
-  Busy (in meeting, deep work)
-  Away (lunch, break, errands)
-  Offline (out of office, end of day)

**Custom Status Examples**:
- "In meeting until 3 PM"
- "Lunch break - back at 1:30 PM"
- "Focus time - DM for urgent matters"
- "OOO - see [backup person] for urgent matters"

**Calendar**:
- Keep calendar updated (shows availability)
- Block focus time (meetings won't be scheduled)
- Set working hours (prevents meetings outside hours)
- Decline meetings that conflict

**Email Signature**:
Include working hours in email signature:
> "I work flexible hours. If you receive this email outside your working hours, please don't feel obligated to respond until you're back."

### 5.4 Overtime & On-Call

**Overtime** (non-exempt only):
- Requires pre-approval
- Paid at 1.5x hourly rate
- Capped at 10 hours/week (unless exceptional circumstances)

**On-Call Rotation** (engineers):
- 1 week every 8 weeks
- Compensation: $500/week + overtime for actual incidents
- Must respond within 15 minutes
- Backup engineer available (escalation)
- Handoff: Monday 9 AM

**On-Call Expectations**:
-  Must be near computer (with internet access)
-  Phone notifications enabled
-  Respond within 15 minutes (acknowledge, provide ETA)
-  Escalate if can't resolve in 1 hour
-  Cannot consume alcohol (may need to respond)
-  Cannot travel without backup coverage arranged

## 6. Performance & Productivity

### 6.1 Performance Expectations

**Same Standards**:
- Remote employees held to same performance standards as office employees
- No distinction in evaluations

**Key Performance Indicators**:
- **Deliverables**: Meeting deadlines, quality of work
- **Communication**: Responsiveness, clarity, proactiveness
- **Collaboration**: Team engagement, code reviews, helping others
- **Initiative**: Identifying improvements, self-directed learning
- **Reliability**: Availability during core hours, attending meetings

### 6.2 Productivity Tracking

**Output-Based** (not time-based):
- Measured by: Completed tasks, shipped features, resolved tickets
- Not measured by: Hours logged, activity indicators, mouse movements

**Tools Used**:
- Jira (task completion, velocity)
- GitHub (commits, PRs, code reviews)
- Sprint goals (team capacity planning)
- Quarterly OKRs (individual goals)

**No Surveillance Software**:
- Company does NOT use:
  - Keystroke loggers
  - Screenshot tools
  - Mouse movement trackers
  - Time tracking software (except for non-exempt)
- We trust employees to manage their time

### 6.3 Productivity Tips

**Focus Time**:
- Block 2-4 hours daily for deep work (no meetings)
- Use Pomodoro technique (25 min work, 5 min break)
- Turn off notifications during focus time
- Communicate focus time to team (Slack status)

**Task Management**:
- Prioritize: Use Eisenhower Matrix (urgent/important)
- Break large tasks into small chunks (< 4 hours)
- Tackle hardest task first (morning energy peak)
- Close open loops (finish before starting new)

**Environment Optimization**:
- Noise: Use noise-canceling headphones or white noise
- Temperature: Keep room cool (68-72°F optimal)
- Lighting: Natural light + task lighting
- Clutter: Keep desk tidy (reduces cognitive load)

**Boundaries**:
- Physical: Leave workspace at end of day (don't work from bed)
- Temporal: Set hard start/stop times (prevent overwork)
- Mental: Close Slack outside work hours (use "Do Not Disturb")

### 6.4 Addressing Performance Issues

**Early Intervention**:
- Manager reaches out if performance concerns (don't wait for annual review)
- 1-on-1 to discuss: specific issues, expectations, support needed

**Performance Improvement Plan** (if issues persist):
- 60-90 day plan with specific goals
- Weekly check-ins with manager
- Additional support (training, mentorship, resources)
- Outcomes: Improvement → regular status; No improvement → termination

**Remote-Specific Issues**:
- Lack of communication → Set explicit response time expectations
- Missing meetings → Adjust schedule, clarify expectations
- Isolation → Increase 1-on-1 frequency, assign buddy
- Distractions → Discuss home environment, consider hybrid/coworking

## 7. Security & Data Protection

### 7.1 VPN Usage

**Mandatory VPN**:
-  Required when accessing:
  - Company network resources
  - Internal tools (Jira, Confluence, databases)
  - Customer data
  - Code repositories (if not on GitHub.com)
-  Required when on:
  - Public Wi-Fi (coffee shops, libraries, airports)
  - Hotel Wi-Fi
  - Any untrusted network

**VPN Setup**:
- Installed during onboarding (IT assists)
- Auto-connect configured (seamless)
- Split tunneling: Work traffic via VPN, personal traffic direct (faster for streaming, etc.)

**VPN Troubleshooting**:
- Can't connect → Restart VPN client, check internet, contact IT
- Slow performance → Try different VPN server location
- Disconnects frequently → Update VPN client, check router settings

### 7.2 Device Security

**Company Devices**:
- **Encryption**: Full disk encryption (FileVault on Mac, BitLocker on Windows)
- **Passwords**: Strong password (12+ characters) + 2FA
- **Screen Lock**: Auto-lock after 5 minutes idle
- **Updates**: Install security updates within 7 days
- **Antivirus**: Company-provided (Sophos, CrowdStrike)
- **MDM**: Mobile Device Management enrolled (allows remote wipe if lost)

**Physical Security**:
-  Keep laptop secure (don't leave unattended in public)
-  Lock laptop when away (even at home)
-  Don't let family/friends use work laptop
-  Store laptop safely when traveling (hotel safe)
-  Don't leave laptop in car (visible target)

**Lost or Stolen Device**:
1. Report immediately to security@company.com (within 1 hour)
2. IT remotely wipes device (protects company data)
3. File police report (if stolen)
4. Replacement device shipped (next business day)

### 7.3 Data Handling

**Data Classification**:

**Public**:
- Marketing materials, press releases
- Public blog posts, open-source code
- Can share freely

**Internal**:
- Code, technical documentation
- Internal communications (Slack, email)
- Company policies
- Share only with employees

**Confidential**:
- Customer data, PII
- Financial data (revenue, salaries)
- Product roadmaps, business strategies
- Share only on need-to-know basis

**Restricted**:
- Payment card data (PCI)
- Health information (HIPAA)
- Social Security Numbers
- Access requires special authorization

**Handling Guidelines**:
-  Store on company devices only (not personal)
-  Use encrypted tools (Google Drive, not Dropbox personal)
-  Delete when no longer needed (don't hoard)
-  Shred paper documents (don't throw in trash)
-  Don't email sensitive data (use secure file sharing)
-  Don't discuss confidential matters in public (cafes, elevators)
-  Don't store on USB drives (unless encrypted)

### 7.4 Network Security

**Home Network**:
-  Change router default password
-  Use WPA3 encryption (or WPA2 if router older)
-  Disable WPS (security vulnerability)
-  Update router firmware regularly
-  Use guest network for IoT devices (separate from work devices)

**Public Wi-Fi**:
-  Avoid if possible (use phone hotspot instead)
-  If must use: Always connect via VPN
-  Don't access sensitive data (customer records, financial data)
-  Don't log into important accounts (banking, payroll)

### 7.5 Phishing & Social Engineering

**Be Vigilant**:
-  Hover over links before clicking (verify URL)
-  Check sender email carefully (look for typos)
-  Verify requests via secondary channel (if urgent wire transfer request, call person)
-  Report suspicious emails (forward to phishing@company.com)
-  Don't click links in unexpected emails
-  Don't download attachments from unknown senders
-  Don't provide passwords or sensitive info via email

**Common Phishing Examples**:
- "Your password will expire, click here to reset" → Phishing
- "Package delivery failed, click to reschedule" → Phishing
- "Your account has been suspended, verify now" → Phishing
- "CEO urgently needs wire transfer" → Business Email Compromise (verify via phone!)

**If You Click a Phishing Link**:
1. Don't panic
2. Disconnect from internet (Wi-Fi off)
3. Report to security@company.com immediately
4. Change password (after IT confirms safe)
5. IT scans device for malware

## 9. Ergonomics & Health

### 9.1 Ergonomic Setup

**Proper Workstation Setup**:

**Monitor**:
- Top of screen at or slightly below eye level
- 20-30 inches from eyes (arm's length)
- Screen tilted 10-20 degrees back
- Reduce glare (position away from windows, use anti-glare screen)

**Chair**:
- Feet flat on floor (or footrest)
- Knees at 90-degree angle
- Hips slightly higher than knees
- Lumbar support (curve fits lower back)
- Armrests at desk height (shoulders relaxed)

**Keyboard & Mouse**:
- Elbows at 90-degree angle
- Wrists straight (not bent up/down or side-to-side)
- Keyboard directly in front of you (not off to side)
- Mouse close to keyboard (avoid reaching)

**Laptop Users**:
- Use external keyboard + mouse + laptop stand
- OR use external monitor + laptop closed
- Never use laptop on lap (poor posture, overheating)

**Diagram**:
```
        [Monitor at eye level]
              |
    [Keyboard & Mouse]
              |
    [Chair with lumbar]
              |
    [Feet flat on floor]
```

### 9.2 Ergonomic Assessment

**Free Assessment**:
- Request via HR (ergonomics@company.com)
- Conducted virtually (video call with ergonomics specialist)
- Duration: 30 minutes
- Outcome: Recommendations for equipment/adjustments

**When to Request**:
- Experiencing discomfort (neck, back, wrist pain)
- New to remote work (setup assistance)
- Changed workspace
- After injury (return to work accommodations)

**Follow-Up**:
- Equipment recommendations (company purchases)
- Re-assessment in 30 days (verify improvements)

### 9.3 Health & Wellness

**Take Breaks**:
- **20-20-20 Rule**: Every 20 minutes, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds (eye strain)
- **Stretch**: Every hour, stand and stretch (2-5 minutes)
- **Move**: Walk around house, do light exercises
- **Lunch break**: Leave desk, eat away from workspace

**Movement**:
- Walking meetings (take calls while walking)
- Desk exercises (chair yoga, stretches)
- Standing desk or converter (alternate sitting/standing)
- Evening walk or exercise (transition from work mode)

**Mental Health**:
- Combat isolation:
  - Virtual coffee chats (random pairing tool)
  - Join interest-based Slack channels (#running, #gaming, #cooking)
  - Attend company social events (in-person or virtual)
- Set boundaries (see section 6.3)
- Use EAP (6 free counseling sessions)
- Take PTO (don't hoard, mental health important)

**Vision**:
- Annual eye exam (covered by vision insurance)
- Blue light glasses (optional, reimbursed up to $100)
- Proper screen brightness (match room lighting)

## 10. Tax & Legal Considerations

### 10.1 Work Location Restrictions

**Allowed Locations**:
- Primary residence (address on file with HR)
- Temporary locations (travel, visiting family) up to 30 days
- Coworking spaces (within same state)

**Prior Approval Required**:
- Moving to new state (notify HR at least 30 days before)
- Working from different state for >30 days
- Working from another country (see section 12)

**Why Approval Needed**:
- **Tax implications**: Nexus, withholding, multi-state taxation
- **Legal compliance**: Labor laws vary by state
- **Compensation**: Salaries adjusted based on location (cost of living)
- **Equipment**: Shipping, retrieval logistics

### 10.2 Tax Implications

**State Income Tax**:
- Withheld based on work location (not home state in some cases)
- Multi-state workers: May owe taxes to multiple states (consult CPA)
- Company handles withholding (you file returns in April)

**Home Office Deduction**:
- **W-2 Employees**: NOT eligible for home office deduction (TCJA 2017 eliminated)
- **1099 Contractors**: Can claim home office (consult CPA)
- Company reimbursements: NOT taxable (separate from deduction)

**Moving to Different State**:
- Notify HR (update address, tax withholding)
- Salary may be adjusted (lower or higher based on location)
- Example: Moving from SF to Austin → Salary may decrease (lower COL)

### 10.3 Workers' Compensation

**Coverage**:
- Work-from-home injuries covered (if occurred during work hours, performing work duties)
- Examples: Tripping over laptop cable, carpal tunnel from typing

**Reporting Injuries**:
1. Report to manager and HR within 24 hours
2. Fill out injury report (online form)
3. Seek medical treatment (urgent care, ER if serious)
4. Follow up with workers' comp claim (HR assists)

**Not Covered**:
- Injuries during breaks (lunch, personal errands)
- Pre-existing conditions (unless work aggravated)
- Injuries due to intoxication or willful misconduct

### 10.4 Unemployment Insurance

**State UI Tax**:
- Company pays unemployment insurance (you don't pay)
- Based on work location state

**Eligibility**:
- If laid off: File for unemployment (state agency)
- If quit: Generally NOT eligible (exceptions: constructive discharge, unsafe conditions)

## 11. Transitioning Between Arrangements

### 11.1 Remote to Office

**Reasons for Transition**:
- Employee preference (prefers office environment)
- Performance issues (productivity concerns)
- Role change (new role requires office presence)
- Business needs (team co-location, client-facing)

**Process**:
1. Discuss with manager (understand reasons)
2. Determine timeline (typically 30 days notice)
3. HR facilitates (desk assignment, office access)
4. Return remote-specific equipment (monitor, chair if company-provided)
5. Adjust benefits (stop internet reimbursement)

**Transition Support**:
- Gradual: Start 2-3 days/week, increase to 5 (if desired)
- Parking: Company-subsidized parking or transit pass
- Re-acclimation: May feel overwhelming initially (normal)

### 11.2 Office to Remote

**Reasons for Transition**:
- Relocation (moving to area without office)
- Personal circumstances (caregiving, health)
- Performance improvement (fewer distractions)
- Pandemic or emergency (company-initiated)

**Process**:
1. Request via HR portal (follow section 1.3)
2. Trial period (30 days)
3. Home office setup (order equipment)
4. Training (remote work best practices)
5. Ongoing check-ins (weekly for first month)

**Benefits**:
- Internet reimbursement ($50/month)
- Home office stipend ($500 one-time)
- Increased flexibility

### 11.3 Full-Time to Part-Time

**Remote Part-Time**:
- Allowed (20-30 hours/week)
- Prorated PTO, no health insurance (unless >30 hours)
- Flexibility on days/hours (coordinate with team)
- Salary adjusted (prorated)

**Process**:
1. Discuss with manager (ensure role can be part-time)
2. HR approval (benefits impact explained)
3. Update employment agreement (hours, compensation)
4. Effective date (typically 30 days out)

**Returning to Full-Time**:
- Request anytime (business need permitting)
- May need to wait for open req (not automatic)

## 12. International Remote Work

### 12.1 Policy Overview

**General Rule**: International remote work NOT permitted (due to complexity)

**Exceptions** (rare, case-by-case):
- Short-term (< 30 days): May be approved for personal travel
- Long-term (> 30 days): Requires executive approval, legal review, tax implications

**Why Restricted**:
- **Tax**: Permanent establishment, transfer pricing, double taxation
- **Legal**: Labor laws, work permits, visas
- **Compliance**: Data protection (GDPR, CCPA, etc.)
- **Payroll**: Complex multi-country payroll setup
- **Equipment**: Shipping, retrieval, customs
- **Time Zones**: Collaboration challenges

### 12.2 Short-Term International Work

**Permitted** (with approval):
- Working from abroad for up to 30 days/year
- "Workcation" (working while traveling)

**Approval Process**:
1. Submit request at least 30 days before travel
2. Manager approval (consider time zones, collaboration needs)
3. Legal review (certain countries prohibited: China, Russia, Iran, North Korea)
4. Tax review (ensure no permanent establishment risk)
5. IT review (VPN, network access)

**Requirements**:
- Stable internet (minimum 50 Mbps)
- Availability during overlap with team (min 4 hours)
- Same performance standards
- VPN required (secure connection)

**Example**:
- Employee in US wants to work from Portugal for 3 weeks
- Submit request → Approved
- Requirements: Available 1-5 PM EST (6-10 PM Portugal), VPN, daily check-ins

### 12.3 Long-Term International Work

**Permanent Relocation to Another Country**:
- Generally NOT supported (employee would need to resign)
- Exception: Company has entity in that country (e.g., UK, Canada, Germany)
- Process: Transfer to local entity (new employment contract, local benefits)

**Digital Nomad**:
- Rotating between countries every few months
- NOT supported (tax complexity, inconsistent work environment)
- Alternative: Take unpaid leave or sabbatical

**Visa & Immigration**:
- Company does NOT sponsor work permits for remote work abroad
- If you have citizenship/right to work abroad: Discuss with HR (may be possible)

### 12.4 Prohibited Countries

**Cannot Work From** (even short-term):
- China (data restrictions, VPN issues)
- Russia (sanctions, security concerns)
- Iran, North Korea, Syria (sanctions)
- Countries with US sanctions or trade restrictions

**Restricted Countries** (requires VP approval):
- Countries with weak data protection
- Countries with unstable internet infrastructure
- Countries in conflict zones

### Appendix F: Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I work from a coffee shop?**
A: Yes, occasionally (use VPN). Not recommended for confidential work or video calls (privacy concerns).

**Q: Can I work from another state temporarily?**
A: Yes, up to 30 days (notify manager). More than 30 days requires HR approval (tax implications).

**Q: What if my internet goes down?**
A: 1) Use mobile hotspot (backup), 2) Go to coworking space, 3) Notify manager (use PTO if cannot work).

**Q: Do I get a company phone?**
A: You can request one, or use personal phone with $50/month stipend.

**Q: Can I work from abroad?**
A: Short-term (< 30 days) with approval. Long-term generally not permitted.

**Q: Am I required to have video on for meetings?**
A: Internal meetings: yes (builds rapport). Large meetings (>20): optional. External meetings: yes (professional).

**Q: Can I use my personal laptop?**
A: No, company laptop required (security, compliance).

**Q: What if I'm not productive at home?**
A: Discuss with manager. Options: Hybrid arrangement, coworking space, office-based.

**Q: Can my family use my work laptop?**
A: No, company devices for authorized users only.

**Q: Do I need to request PTO to work from a different location?**
A: No, as long as you're working your normal hours (notify manager).
